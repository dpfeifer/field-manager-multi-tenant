## What goes on an invoice

**Who it is from and who it is to.** Your business name and how to reach you, and the customer's name and address. If the customer is a business, use the name they pay bills under.

**A number and two dates.** Number invoices in sequence so you and your customer can refer to one without confusion. The invoice date is when you send it; the due date is when you expect payment. Fourteen days is common for small service work, and "due on receipt" is reasonable for a one-off job.

**One line per thing you did.** A description, a quantity, a rate. "Interior repaint, living room and hall, 2 coats" tells the customer what they paid for; "Painting" does not. Materials you are passing through go on their own lines.

**Tax, discount and deposit.** Add tax only if your state and trade require it on labor or materials. A deposit already paid comes off the total so the amount due is what is actually left.

**How to pay.** Put it in the notes: Venmo handle, Zelle, check made out to whom, a card link. The easier it is to pay, the sooner you are paid.
