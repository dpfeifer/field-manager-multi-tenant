## How the number is worked out

**Billable hours are the whole story.** You might work 45 hours a week, but driving, quoting, supply runs and paperwork are not billed to anyone. For most solo operators somewhere between half and three quarters of working hours are billable. Your rate has to cover all the hours from only the billable ones.

**The sum.** Add the pay you want to your yearly business costs. Divide by the billable hours in your year. Then divide by one minus your profit margin, so the business keeps something after paying you.

**What counts as a business cost.** Vehicle, fuel, insurance, tools and equipment, materials you do not bill through, phone, software, licences, accounting, advertising. If you are self-employed in the US, remember that self-employment tax comes out of your pay: set the pay figure with that in mind.

**A rate is not a price.** For jobs you quote as a fixed price, estimate the hours, multiply by this rate, add materials, and quote that. The customer sees a price; you know it covers your time.
