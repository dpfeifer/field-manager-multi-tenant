## What makes an estimate get accepted

**Be specific about the work.** One line per task, with enough detail that the customer can picture it: "Pressure wash driveway and front walk, approx. 900 sq ft" rather than "Pressure washing". Specific estimates get fewer questions and fewer disputes later.

**Separate labor from materials.** Customers understand a $400 materials line next to a $900 labor line better than a single $1,300 number, and it protects you if material prices move.

**Put a date on it.** An estimate that is valid for 30 days gives the customer a reason to decide, and gives you a way out if costs change. The generator fills in 30 days from today; change it if you like.

**Say what happens next.** Use the notes for your terms: deposit required to schedule, how the final invoice may differ, what is not included. Short and plain beats legal language.

**Leave room for a yes.** The signature and date lines at the bottom turn the estimate into an agreement when the customer signs it.

## When a generator stops being enough

A generator is fine for the odd quote. Once you are sending a few a week, the retyping starts: the same customer into the estimate, then into your calendar, then into the invoice.
